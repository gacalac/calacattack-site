  • Nóon is nóo plus the equative suffix -n. The suffix goes on the first stressed word of the sentence and is used only after a vowel or a y. After any other consonant it drops: Konókniyk nóo pó'kwaq.
  • The present forms can stand alone with a noun or adjective: Nóon 'atáax. (I am a human being.)
  • The past forms need míyquş after the noun or adjective: Nóonil 'atáax míyquş. (I was a human being.)
  • To ask a question, drop the suffix and add the question word: Nóo şun 'éxmawish? (Am I dirty?), 'Óm şu no'ó'nan? (Are you my friend?), Cháam şush páapavichum? (Are we thirsty?)
  • With plural subjects the adjective takes a plural form, such as háqmawichum (hungry, animate plural).

Verbs: how they work, and every verb in the app
  • Is -ing, one subject: -q on every verb.
  • Are -ing, several people: a-verbs take -an. Type A, i-verbs and other verbs take -wun.
  • Was / were -ing: -quş on every verb.
  • Simple past: a-verbs drop a and add -ya. Type A verbs usually add -x, and some reduplicate instead. i-verbs drop i and add -yax. Other verbs add -x after u or o, and -ax after a consonant.
  • Will: a-verbs and Type A verbs add -an. i-verbs and verbs ending in u or o add -n.
  • Will be -ing: a-verbs add -xmaan. All others add -màan (or -maan after a consonant).
  • Command, to one / to several: a-verbs add -x / -xam. All others add nothing / -yam.
  • Wants to: a-verbs add -xvichuq. All others add -vichuq.

Put qáy before a verb for “not.” For “don’t” in a command, put Tóşşu (to one person) or Tóşşum (to several) before the command form. An a-verb ends in a. A Type A verb also ends in a but acts like the other classes; Book 7 names the common ones, and any verb ending in 'na or in a consonant (not l) plus la is Type A. About 90% of other verbs ending in a are plain a-verbs. Rules from Books 5, 7 and 8.

Nouns: possession, plurals, endings, and “to have”
  • The noun suffix drops. Most nouns end in a noun suffix (-sh, -t, -l, -la, -cha, -ta). It drops before a possessive prefix or before any of the endings below: táanat (rug) becomes notáana (my rug) and táananga (on the rug).
  • Possessive prefixes: no- my, 'o- your, po- his / her, cham- our, 'om- your guys’, pom- their. Family members and body parts are always possessed.
  • Possessed noun as an object: add -i after a consonant and -y after a vowel (notáanay). A short stressed vowel before the final consonant lengthens (notúng becomes notúungi).
  • Endings: -nga in / on / at, -ngay from, -yuk to (-yk after a vowel, -ik after n), -tal with / using (-chal is also possible after i, y or x; a short stressed vowel lengthens, as in kúutal), -man along with.
  • Animate nouns (people and animals) take none of those endings. Their plural adds -um, -chum (from -sh) or -vuktum (from -kat), and a short stressed vowel in the last syllable lengthens (pachát becomes pacháatum). Their object adds -i (or -y after a vowel); the plural object adds -mi.
  • Possessing animate nouns: there is no way to say “my dog” or “my man.” Animals use 'áshla (no'áash 'awáal, my dog; cham'áachum 'awáalum, our dogs). People use 'ó'nan “friend” (no'ó'nan ya'ásh, my friend, the man). “One who does” nouns use compounds (néy~húu'unikat, my teacher).
  • -ki possession (Book 8, Lessons 6 and 8): nouns ending in -aat and a group of nature words drop the noun suffix and add -ki: chóoraat becomes nochóoraanaki (my coin), qawíicha becomes poqawíiki, matámtash becomes nomatámtaki. A few have special shapes (notemékki, noşú'laki, nonávki).
  • Verbs as nouns (Lesson 7): add -sh after a vowel other than a (tíiwi → tíiwish) and after Type A verbs, -xish after a plain a-verb (héela → héelaxish, “a song”), and -ish after a consonant (háal → háalish).
  • A noun possessing a noun (Lesson 14): put the possessor first in its plain form and give the possessed noun po- (or pom- if there is more than one possessor): ya'ásh ponáq (the man's ear), hengé'malum pomnáq (the boys' ears), 'oyó' po'ó'nan pokúun (your mother's friend's bag). A possessor can also possess an adjective (ya'ásh pomíx yót, the man's big one), and 'óonop ya'ásh pomíx means "that is the man's."
  • "To have" (Lesson 16): there is no verb. The possessor is the prefix and the verb says the thing is there. Animate: 'áawq (one) or qálwun (several); not having: 'ómmaq / 'ómmaan. Inanimate: qáala (one) or wónq (several); míyq for intangible or mass nouns; şé'q / wáachaq for things standing; not having: yáawaq. Example: nopiwípwila qáala, "I have a pencil."
  • Inanimate nouns have no plural form. As objects (Book 6, Lesson 13) -aat becomes -aanti, and -cha, -ta and -la drop the vowel after a long vowel: kíicha becomes kíish, tóota becomes tóot, páala becomes páal. After a consonant they add a helping vowel: pálvunla becomes pálvunil, and a short stressed vowel lengthens (kúnla becomes kúunil, şú'la becomes şúu'ul). Five nouns use u instead of i ('ówla becomes 'óowul).
  • Object pronouns (Book 6, Lesson 12): néy me, 'óy you, póy him / her, cháami us, 'omóomi you guys, pomóomi them. this and that before an animate object: 'ivíiyi, 'óonomi, ponéeyi, wunáali.

From Books 3, 5 and 8 (Lessons 1 to 13). Object forms of other inanimate nouns are taught in Book 6, which is not in the app.

Numbers: how Luiseño counts
  • 0 to 10: qayhíycha, supúl, wéh, páahay, wasá', maháar, paváahay, kavíkvish, şíw'lash, nomaawasá', táppaat.
  • 11 to 19: táppaat + the digit + pa'áq “on top”: táppaat wéh pa'áq (12).
  • Tens: the digit + -kun “times” + táppaat: wéhkun táppaat (20), and wéhkun táppaat supúl pa'áq (21).
  • Hundreds: şóttaat (100, never “one hundred”), wéhkun şóttaat (200). Digits after a hundred come with no pa'áq (şóttaat supúl, 101), but teens and tens keep it.
  • Thousands: míil (1,000), şóttaat míil (100,000), then the rest.
  • As an object: -aat becomes -aanti (táppaanti, şóttaanti), and zero is qayhísh.
  • People and animals have their own numerals (Book 7, Lesson 6; Book 6, Lesson 31): supúl (object supúuli), wéhchum, páachum, wasáa'um, maháarum, paváahayum, kavíkvichum, şíw'lachum, nomaawasáa'um, táppaantum. Objects add -i or -mi (wéhchumi, kavíkvishmi). Higher numbers pluralize táppaat and şóttaat (táppaantum, şóttaantum) and the digits; the -kun words stay the same: kavíkvishkun táppaantum wéhchum pa'áq (72).
  • Counting by: súspultal (ones), wéewihtal (twos), páavaytal, wawsá'tal, mamháartal (fives), táptappaantal (tens), şótşottaantal (hundreds).

From Books 3 and 5.

Questions: question words and the marker
  • Who: háx (several: 'axím); as an object 'axíiyi (several: 'axíimi). What: híycha (several: híycham); as an object hísh (several: híshmi). Híyq and híx are the verbs “what is” and “say what.”
  • Why: híyngay, literally “from what.”
  • Where: michá' (at), micháy (from where), michíyk (to where). When: míykinga.
  • Which: michát (michát kíicha, which house), micháatum (which ones), micháati and michátmi (as animate objects).
  • How many / how much: hík for things, followed by the noun (hík 'áa'alvish); híkchum for several people or animals, with the object form híkchumi (Híkchum 'amáayum şum qálwun? “How many children are there?”). What day / month / season: híknga, híknga móynga, híknga táwpamaynga.
  • The marker: a small word follows the first word or phrase of the question: şu, or şun for “I,” şush for “we,” şum for “they” and “you guys.” The books spell it both su and şu.
  • Examples: Michá' şu hengéemal 'áawq? (Where is the boy?), Míykinga şu 'óm wukó''aq? (When are you arriving?), Háx şu 'óono? (Who is that?), Nóo şun 'éxmawish? (Am I dirty?).
  • Time: numbers take the endings too: kavíkvinga “at seven,” táppaananga supúlnga pa'áq “at eleven.”

From Book 7, Lesson 5 (Interrogative Words), and Book 4.
